# The Rhythm Control Fallacy

**A Critical Re-Examination of Catheter Ablation for Atrial Fibrillation in the
Era of Direct Oral Anticoagulants**

A critical appraisal of the evidence for catheter ablation in atrial fibrillation
(AF), separating its **well-established symptomatic benefit** from claimed
**prognostic** benefit. It argues the rhythm-centric "sinus rhythm = cure" model
is not supported for the general AF population in RCTs (CABANA ITT: HR 0.86, 95%
CI 0.65–1.15), that meta-analyses showing mortality benefit are driven by
observational data and selection bias, and that clear prognostic benefit is
confined to heart-failure-with-reduced-EF patients (CASTLE-AF, CABANA HF subgroup).
Anticoagulation should continue per CHA₂DS₂-VASc regardless of ablation success.

## Nature of this work

This is a **narrative critical review with quantitative NNT/NNH analysis**, not a
registered systematic review. Effect estimates are taken from the cited trials and
published meta-analyses (notably CABANA 2019 and the Crawford 2024 mediation
analysis); see the methods section for sources and assumptions. Hypothesis-
generating and educational — it reframes ablation as symptom control with
HF-specific prognostic benefit, not universal stroke prevention.
